Action Schema:

Treat the signed delta numbers in the Action Schema as prompt-only amplitude hints for the acting NPC: positive means likely improvement, negative means likely cost or penalty. They are not runtime settlement and not guaranteed results.

Priority Rules:
1. The current dominant motive has the highest internal decision weight for this step.
2. Hard physical feasibility and recent failure feedback may override the dominant motive only when they make the preferred action impossible or invalid.
3. Immediate feasibility overrides routine role behavior, static identity, and long-term goals.
4. Identity, lifestyle, and future goals may only break ties between currently feasible immediate options.
5. Do not weigh all information equally.
6. If a target appears in InvalidTargets, it is forbidden for this immediate step.

Strategic Framing:
- Do not reduce decision-making to matching the lowest motive with the most obvious repair action.
- Before choosing, reason internally about control, leverage, timing, and opportunity cost under the current physical constraints.
- Ask which person, place, object, or access point currently controls the dominant need, and whether approaching it indirectly creates a better position than the most obvious direct repair.
- Ask whether a socially intelligent move such as requesting, trading, coordinating, pressuring, avoiding, repositioning, or securing access creates a more reliable next step than direct consumption or passive waiting.
- Ask what opportunity would be lost by taking the most obvious immediate relief right now.
- Prefer immediate moves that both relieve the dominant pressure and improve control, access, leverage, or future reliability when they remain physically feasible.
- A short, physically feasible detour is acceptable when it creates a meaningfully stronger next step; do not take such a detour if the dominant motive is in immediate critical danger.

Task: What is the next immediate feasible action for !<INPUT 2>! under the current physical constraints?
Output Requirements:
- Write in !<INPUT 2>!'s first-person voice.
- Write one short planning paragraph, not just a single sentence.
- The first sentence must state the next immediate feasible action clearly.
- You may include brief near-term strategic follow-up reasoning, but keep the paragraph centered on the immediate next move rather than a long multi-step script.
- Mention only one primary immediate target object, location, or person for the next action.
- Make the most urgent current internal need explicit.
- Prioritize the dominant motive instead of weighing all information equally.
- If the dominant motive is mood, prefer an immediate action that directly repairs mood unless hard physical constraints block that option.
- If the previous immediate action failed or the target was unreachable, choose a new immediate approach instead of repeating the failed target.
- Fall back to a secondary option only when the dominant motive's preferred action is blocked by physical infeasibility or the latest failure feedback.
